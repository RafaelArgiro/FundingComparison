What is a Simulated Funded Account?
Simulated Funded account: trades mirror live markets with simulated capital and profits can earn real payouts. Risk managers monitor rules, risk and consistency before a possible move to live trading.

Updated over 3 weeks ago
A Simulated Funded Account is a type of trading account designed to replicate the conditions of a live trading environment but without using actual capital.



When you're a newly funded trader, transitioning to live trading can be challenging, and a Simulated Funded Account allows you to gain experience and build confidence in a real-time setting.



This account lets you continue trading with the same level of intensity as a live account while giving you the opportunity to demonstrate consistency in your trading performance.





How Does it Work?
In a Simulated Funded Account, your trades mirror what would happen in the live markets, but your profits and losses are not real. This setup gives you the chance to practice your strategies and manage risks as if you were trading with real money.



The profits you generate in the Simulated Funded Account can still lead to actual payouts, allowing you to earn while you hone your skills.





What's the Purpose?
The primary goal of the Simulated Funded Account is to provide a buffer period where you can adapt to the psychological and strategic demands of live trading.



It helps bridge the gap between being a successful evaluation trader and becoming a consistent performer in live markets.





Monitoring and Evaluation
Throughout your time in the Simulated Funded Account, our risk managers will closely monitor your trading activity. They assess your ability to follow trading rules, manage risk, and maintain consistency.



Once you demonstrate that you can trade successfully within the parameters of the Simulated Funded Account, you may be transitioned to a full live trading account where you'll trade with real capital.

Drawdown Protection Types
What is a Trailing Drawdown? Understanding a trailing drawdown involves knowing it as a dynamic loss limit mechanism. It calculates maximum allowable losses "trailing" or adjusting with your account's peak value, integrating into Tradeify's risk management strategy.



A trailing drawdown is your account's maximum permissible loss limit. The limit trails your highest end-of-day balance (the high water mark) and only moves up, never down. It is enforced in real time against your net liquidation value, which includes unrealized P&L on open positions - breaching this limit even momentarily results in account failure, reinforcing disciplined trading practices.

Recalculates only at market close based on your final daily balance

Provides flexibility during the trading day - temporary intraday losses do not move your drawdown limit. The limit itself is still enforced in real time, and touching it fails the account immediately

Example (Sim Funded only): A 50K Growth funded account with a $2,000 trailing drawdown locks its floor at $50,100 once its end-of-day balance reaches $52,100. Drawdown never locks on evaluation accounts.

Guidelines for Traders
Trader conduct rules: Good Faith policy, hedging ban incl. Product Groups and across accounts, bots and algo ownership, microscalping 10-second payout rule, one trade per week idle rule, news trading, DCA allowed.

Updated over 3 weeks ago
At Tradeify, the rules and policies we've established are designed not to restrict your trading, but to create a foundation that maximizes profitability and supports long-term success.



Our goal is to leverage your talent, manage risk effectively, and foster a sustainable trading environment where both the firm and traders benefit.





Good Faith Policy
The core of Tradeify's guidelines is a mutual understanding between Tradeify and the trader that the trader acts in good faith. Therefore traders are prohibited from using strategies that seek to exploit or create errors within our platform.



This includes taking advantage of discrepancies in price displays, delays in updates, or any other technical errors in our services.



Such actions are considered unethical and can lead to serious consequences, including termination of your trading account.



By adhering to these guidelines, we can work together to build a mutually advantageous relationship that drives success for all.





Hedging


Hedging is prohibited. You cannot hold opposing positions — one long, one short — on the same instrument or on two products from the same Product Group, whether those positions sit in a single account or are spread across several of your accounts.



A hedging violation results in every account involved being set to violation status, forfeiture of any profits generated during the period of the violating activity, and possibly a permanent ban from Tradeify.



For the full rule, including the Product Groups table and worked examples, see Rules: Hedging & Correlated Products.





Bots/Algorithmic Trading
At Tradeify, we allow the use of bots and algorithms under certain conditions:

Ownership: You must be able to prove that you are the sole owner of the bot or strategy, and that no one else has access to or is using it. This ensures that the bot/algorithm is not being shared with other traders or firms.

We scan to ensure there are no similar orders on other accounts. We will also require a live video of you enabling the code on your own PC.

Exclusive Use: While you may use the bot on your personal accounts, using it across multiple firms is against Tradeify's policy. The bot should be solely for your own use within Tradeify.

No High-Frequency Trading (HFT) Bots: Personal bots are allowed as long as they are not high-frequency trading (HFT) bots. Tradeify has specific risk measures in place to detect and monitor such activities.

Compliance and Verification: Tradeify reserves the right to request information or documentation if our risk measures flag your account for potential violations of these guidelines.



These rules are in place to maintain the integrity of trading on our platform and ensure fairness for all traders.





Microscalping
Microscalping refers to executing trades with the intent of capturing small profits from minimal price movements, such as a few ticks or points typically within a very short period of time.



This rule applies to funded accounts only. It does not apply during an evaluation.



Tradeify is looking for traders that are serious about their trading and can be called up to live to eventually be copied by our own firm as well.



Trades executed in less than 10 seconds are difficult to reliably replicate or copy.



To request a payout on a funded account, you must meet BOTH of the below two criteria:

Over 50% of your trades are longer than 10 seconds

Over 50% of your profit must come from trades held longer than 10 seconds.



If you do not meet the criteria, you will not be able to request a payout. It does not fail your account.





Maximum Account Idle Time (Funded and Evaluation Accounts)
To keep your funded or evaluation account active, you must place at least one trade per week (Monday through Friday). This is per account, not per user.



Failure to do so may result in your account being marked as inactive. If we are concerned about your max idle time we will message you before we take any action.





News Trading, Dollar Cost Averaging, Flipping, and Scaling
We do not have any rules against or guidelines around trading news events, Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA), flipping, or scaling.



Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) refers to entering a trade with multiple executions at different price points from the initial entry.



DCA is allowed, but it should always be part of a structured strategy. It is discouraged to "average into oblivion" involving continuously adding to a losing position without a clear strategy, until the trade reaches breakeven or a slight profit.

What is a Trading Day?
A trading day (market session) runs 6:00 PM to 5:00 PM ET, so trades at 10 AM and 7 PM count as two days. Payouts need the 10-second rule. Rithmic holiday half-days are recorded at the next full close.

Updated over 3 weeks ago
A trading day is counted as at least one trade placed from 6:00 P.M. ET to 5:00 P.M. ET the next day.



Tradeify users should know that trading days are a critical part of the system, consolidated from 6:00 P.M. ET to 5:00 P.M. ET on the following day. It aligns trades and provides clarity on account processing.



The term can cause confusion, so it may be easier to think of it as "Market Session" instead.



To qualify for a payout in Tradeify, users must fulfill trading activity requirements, including the 10-second rule on funded accounts: over 50% of trades held longer than 10 seconds and over 50% of profit from those trades.



For example, if you placed a trade at 10:00 A.M ET and then another at 7:00 P.M ET on the same day, you might think that that's one "trading day," but it's actually two, because the trades fall on two different market sessions.



Rithmic accounts: On the Rithmic broker, a holiday half-day (shortened session) is not recorded until the next full market close. The day still counts as a trading day, but the credit (and any payout based on it) may not post until the following full session closes. For example, a Friday holiday half-day is recorded at the following Monday's market close.

Understanding Order Types: The Foundation
Order types are your primary tools for controlling when, how, and at what price your trades execute. Choosing the right order type can mean the difference between a profitable trade and a costly mistake.



Why Order Types Matter:

Price Control: Get the fills you want, not just any fill

Risk Management: Protect your account from unexpected market moves

Trade Automation: Execute your strategy even when you're not watching

Slippage Reduction: Minimize the difference between expected and actual prices

Strategy Execution: Implement complex trading plans with precision





Market Orders: Immediate Execution


What Market Orders Do
Market orders execute immediately at the best available price in the market. When you submit a market order, you're saying "fill me now, whatever the current price."





When to Use Market Orders
Perfect for:

Quick entries when you see a setup developing

Fast exits when you need out of a position immediately

High-volume markets with tight bid-ask spreads (ES, NQ, YM)

News reactions where speed matters more than price

Stop-loss execution when protecting capital is priority

Example Scenario: You see a strong breakout forming on ES and want immediate entry. A market order gets you in within milliseconds rather than risking the move continue without you in a trade.





Market Order Advantages
Guaranteed execution (assuming market is open)

Immediate fills with no waiting

Simple to use - just click buy or sell

Perfect for liquid markets where spreads are tight





Market Order Risks & Considerations
Price uncertainty - you don't control the exact fill price

Slippage risk in fast-moving or thin markets

Gap risk if market jumps between when you click and when order fills

Not recommended for low-volume contracts or wide spreads

Risk Management Tip: Always check the bid-ask spread before using market orders.





Limit Orders: Price Control


What Limit Orders Do
Limit orders let you specify the exact price (or better) at which you want your order filled. Buy limits go below current market price, sell limits go above current market price.





When to Use Limit Orders
Perfect for:

Precise entries at key support/resistance levels

Profit-taking at predetermined target prices

Better fills in choppy or ranging markets

Cost averaging strategies where price matters most

Overnight orders where you want specific price execution

Example Scenario: ES is trading at 4,500 but you want to buy at the 4,485 support level. A buy limit at 4,485 will only fill if the market comes down to your price or better.





Limit Order Execution Details
Fill Priorities:

Price priority: Better prices fill first

Time priority: Earlier orders at same price fill first

Size considerations: Large orders may receive partial fills

Important Notes:

Market must trade through your limit price to guarantee fills

Partial fills possible if insufficient volume at your price

Orders remain active until filled, canceled, or expired





Stop Orders: Protection and Momentum


What Stop Orders Do
Stop orders become market orders when the market reaches your specified stop price. They're placed opposite to current market direction: buy stops above market, sell stops below market.





When to Use Stop Orders
For Risk Management:

Stop losses to limit downside on existing positions

Trailing protection as positions move in your favor

Account protection from catastrophic losses

Example Scenarios:

Protection: Long ES at 4,500, place sell stop at 4,480 to limit losses

Momentum: ES consolidating at 4,500, place buy stop at 4,510 to enter breakouts





Stop Order Execution Process
Order placed at your specified stop price

Market reaches your stop price (order becomes "elected")

Converts to market order and fills at next available price

Fill confirmation received (may differ from stop price due to slippage)





Stop Order Risks & Management
Slippage Considerations:

Fast markets: May fill significantly away from stop price

Gap opens: Potential for large slippage on overnight gaps

Low liquidity: Wide fills possible in thin markets

Best Practices:

Account for slippage when setting stop levels

Monitor volatility - wider stops in volatile conditions

Consider stop-limits if slippage control is critical

Avoid round numbers where many stops cluster





Stop-Limit Orders: Slippage Control


What Stop-Limit Orders Do
Stop-limit orders combine stop and limit functionality. When your stop price is hit, a limit order (not market order) is placed at your specified limit price.





When to Use Stop-Limit Orders
Perfect for:

Volatile markets where slippage is a major concern

News events where prices gap significantly

Large positions where slippage costs add up

Precise exit prices where you'd rather miss than pay too much

Example Scenario: Long ES at 4,500, want stop protection at 4,480, but don't want to sell below 4,475. Set stop at 4,480, limit at 4,475.





Stop-Limit Advantages
Slippage protection - never worse than your limit price

Price certainty if filled

Risk control in volatile conditions

Professional execution for larger accounts





Stop-Limit Risks & Trade-offs
The Risk:No fill guarantee - market may blow through your limit price

Position remains open despite stop price being hit

Could result in larger losses than planned

Requires active monitoring and backup plans

Decision Framework:

Use stop-limits when slippage control is more important than fill certainty

Use regular stops when getting out is more important than price paid

Consider market conditions - stop-limits riskier in trending markets





Trailing Stops: Automated Profit Protection


What Trailing Stops Do
Trailing stops automatically adjust your stop loss as the market moves in your favor. They maintain a fixed distance from the best price achieved, locking in profits while allowing for continued upside.





When to Use Trailing Stops
Perfect for:

Trend following strategies where you want to ride momentum

Profit protection without capping upside potential

Breakeven stops that adjust as position becomes profitable

Automated management when you can't monitor constantly

Example Scenario: Long ES at 4,500, set 20-point trailing stop. If ES rises to 4,530, your stop automatically moves from 4,480 to 4,510, protecting 10 points of profit.





Trailing Stop Configuration
Key Settings:

Trail amount: Distance stop maintains from favorable price (in ticks/points)

Activation price: Price level where trailing begins (optional)

Step size: Minimum move required before stop adjusts

Trail Amount Strategy:

Tight trails (10-15 points): Quick profit taking, more whipsaws

Wide trails (25-50 points): Ride trends longer, fewer false exits

Volatility-based: Adjust trail distance based on recent market volatility





Trailing Stop Best Practices
Setup Considerations:

Start conservatively with wider trails while learning

Consider market personality - ES needs different trails than CL

Account for typical pullbacks in your chosen instrument

Test different trail amounts to find what works for your style

Management Tips:

Monitor initial moves to ensure trailing activates properly

Have backup plans if trailing stop doesn't trigger as expected

Consider manual adjustment in unusual market conditions

Review trail settings regularly based on changing volatility





OCO Orders: Complete Position Management


What OCO (One-Cancels-Other) Orders Do
OCO orders link two orders together - when one fills, the other automatically cancels. Most commonly used to set both profit target and stop loss simultaneously.





When to Use OCO Orders
Perfect for:

Complete exit strategy set at position entry

Bracket trading with predetermined profit/loss levels

Risk-reward planning with clear targets and stops

Automated management reducing emotional decision-making

Example Scenario: Enter long ES at 4,500. Set OCO with sell limit at 4,525 (profit target) and sell stop at 4,480 (stop loss). Whichever hits first fills and cancels the other.





OCO Order Strategy Applications
Risk-Reward Ratios:

1:1 Ratio: 25-point target, 25-point stop

2:1 Ratio: 40-point target, 20-point stop

Custom ratios: Based on technical analysis and market conditions

Entry Types with OCO:

Market entry + OCO exits: Immediate position with preset management

Limit entry + OCO exits: Wait for pullback, then manage automatically

Stop entry + OCO exits: Breakout entry with complete management plan





OCO Order Benefits
Eliminates emotion from exit decisions

Ensures position management even when unavailable to monitor

Forces planning of both profit and loss scenarios upfront

Professional approach used by systematic traders





OSO Orders: Sequential Order Logic


What OSO (One-Sends-Other) Orders Do
OSO orders create a sequence: when the first order fills, the second order automatically submits to the market. Used for complex multi-step strategies.





When to Use OSO Orders
Perfect for:

Entry + management sequences: Position entry triggers automatic management

Multi-leg strategies: Complex trades requiring specific order sequences

Scaling strategies: Initial fill triggers additional orders

Automation: Reducing manual intervention in complex strategies

Example Scenario: Set OSO where buy limit at 4,490 (first order) automatically sends OCO bracket (profit target + stop loss) when filled.





OSO Combined with OCO
Complete Automation Strategy:

OSO parent: Buy limit at support level

OCO children: Profit target + stop loss sent when parent fills

Result: Fully automated entry and exit strategy

This combination creates "set and forget" trading where entire strategies execute without manual intervention.

Scaling In and Out
Scaling In:

Multiple limit orders at different price levels

Builds position size as price moves favorably

Reduces average cost basis through better entries

Scaling Out:

Multiple limit orders for profit-taking

Takes profits incrementally as targets are reached

Balances profit-taking with trend-riding

Gems for purchases
Buying an evaluation, funded account, or reset credits Gems automatically, every time, with no cap and no daily limit. Purchase Gems scale with what you spend, so a large order pays a large amount, and none of it counts toward the daily or weekly mission totals. A Growth 25K evaluation pays 100 Gems, a Lightning 25K funded account pays 400 Gems, and a Lightning 150K funded account pays 1,000 Gems. Resets pay between 75 and 250 Gems depending on account size.



The exact amount for each product is shown on its card under the Purchases filter in the Missions tab.





What counts for the journaling, sharing, and Discord missions
Journaling. Session Scribe counts notes in the journal on your trader dashboard. One note for the day is all it takes, and it credits the moment you write it.



Sharing on social. Brand Boost is for posting a Tradeify post, or your own Tradeify content, on social media — up to two a day, one per network. It pays from a link to your post, and the team verifies it before it credits, so it will not appear the second you hit publish.



Sharing your trader card. Show Your Cards is a separate monthly mission. Share your trader card through the share sheet, by copying the link, or straight to X, LinkedIn, Instagram, or Discord. Any completed share counts, once per calendar month, credited instantly.



Discord. Quality Chatter needs five real messages of at least 20 characters in the Tradeify Discord, spread across five different hours of the day. The community bot counts them, usually within a few minutes. Connect your Discord first or the messages will not be seen.





When missions credit
Missions credit at six different times, and every mission card states which one applies to it:



Instant. Profile, leaderboard name, Discord connect, social follows, the daily journal note, and sharing your trader card. The card reads "Credited the moment you complete it."

Within a few minutes. The Discord activity mission, counted by the community bot.

After the trading day closes. In the Game is counted from your daily trade summary once the trading day closes, usually by 23:45 UTC. Green Print and Win Streak need the day's P&L to be final, so they count after midnight UTC, usually by 00:15 UTC. If you close a trade at lunchtime and check the mission an hour later, it has not been counted yet.

Within about half an hour. Milestone events — evaluation passes, approved payouts, Live promotions, a 150K funding — credit within an hour of the event reaching your account data, usually about 30 minutes.

After a manual check. The social sharing mission pays from a link to your post and is verified by the team before it credits.

Settled at the end of the month. The monthly missions (Top 100, Top 10, King of the Hill, Hold the Line) are decided from the final standings once the month closes in UTC, and settle usually within 15 minutes of the close — in practice, right at the start of the first day of the new month. They show as pending all month until then.



Rewards earned during end-of-day reconciliation: Tradeify's reconciliation process runs roughly 5:00 PM to 6:00 PM ET. A reward that lands in that window can take up to an hour to become claimable. Nothing is lost — wait an hour and check again.





Claiming your rewards
Some missions credit the reward straight into your balance. Others hold it until you press Claim on the mission card. Anything waiting to be claimed also appears in the dropdown under the level and Gems pill in the dashboard header.



Unclaimed rewards are never lost. They stay there until you claim them.





Selling a prize you don’t want
Not every pull is going to fit your account — you can land a free reset for an account type you don’t hold, or a prize that just isn’t useful to you. When that happens, you don’t have to keep it. You can sell it back for a partial Gems refund.

You have 24 hours from the moment you open the pack to sell that prize. The sell option sits right on the card in your Vault and disappears once the 24 hour window closes.



Selling returns 60% of the Gems you spent on the pack that awarded it. The refund is based on the pack’s cost, not an individual value assigned to the prize itself.



After 24 hours, the sell option is gone. The prize is yours to keep or redeem whenever you like — unredeemed prizes don’t expire, so there’s no rush if you’d rather hold onto it instead of selling.



Example: you pull a free reset for an account type you don’t currently hold. You can leave it sitting in your Vault for whenever you do hold that account type, since it never expires, or sell it back within the 24 hour window for 60% of the Gems that pack cost you.

Every pack shows its pull rates per rarity and lists every prize in the pool with its individual chance, before you spend a single Gem. The result of an open is determined server-side at the moment you open the pack and cannot be changed afterward.



TradePacks are a game of chance. You can open a Diamond Hands pack and pull a Common — that is the mechanic working correctly, and that Common is still a stronger prize than a Paper Common would have been. Rarity odds apply to every open independently: there is no pity timer and no guarantee after a certain number of opens. A higher-tier pack improves your odds on the best prizes and raises the floor on the rest; it does not promise a top-rarity pull. Pack opens cannot be reversed or refunded.





The Vault: where your prizes live
Every prize you pull lands in the Vault. The header strip counts your cards, how many are still redeemable, your best pull, and how many packs you have opened. Filters split your collection by rarity and by Unredeemed versus Redeemed.






How to redeem a prize you have won
Click a card in the Vault to open it and reveal its code, then press Copy code. Paste that code at checkout on the Tradeify site — not inside the Rewards Center.




Four rules apply to every prize code:



Single use. Each code can be redeemed exactly once. After that, the card shows as Redeemed in your Vault.

No expiry. Prize codes do not expire. A card can sit in your Vault until you are ready to use it.

Locked to you. The code is issued against your own account and can only be redeemed by the email address that won it. It is refused for anyone else, so do not share it.

Scoped to eligible products. A discount code works on standard evaluation, funded account, and reset purchases. It is deliberately not valid on bundled or promotional package offers, which are already discounted, so the code is refused there. That refusal is expected behavior, not a broken code.





Giveaway codes
Under the pack shelf in the Shop there is a Have a giveaway code? link. Codes handed out through campaigns, community events, and promotions are entered there. A valid giveaway code grants a pack and opens it immediately at no Gems cost — you get the same reveal you would have had if you had paid, and the prize lands in your Vault as normal.



Giveaway codes and Vault prize codes are different things. A giveaway code is entered in the Rewards Center to win something. A Vault prize code is entered at checkout on the Tradeify site to redeem something you have already won.





The Leaderboard and seasons
The Leaderboard ranks traders by XP under their chosen public name. It never shows your real name or account details. It has two views: Season (XP earned in the current season) and All-Time. Your own rank is pinned at the top of the page, and the top three appear as podium cards with their Gems prizes.




The monthly leaderboard missions settle once the calendar month closes in UTC, usually within 15 minutes of the close, which in practice means the first day of the new month.



Public names are screened automatically for inappropriate language. If you would rather not appear by name at all, you can turn off showing your name on the rankings from your Rewards Center profile.





How seasons work
The program runs in seasons. Each season carries its own mission catalog, pack line-up, prize pool, and leaderboard.



Nothing you have earned is reset by a new season. Your Gems balance, your XP, your level, and every card in your Vault carry straight over. What resets is the season leaderboard: when a new season starts, season XP goes back to zero for everyone and the climb to the top starts again. Your all-time XP and all-time rank are unaffected. The monthly leaderboard missions settle against the calendar month, independently of the season.

Rewards Center FAQs
Why does my trading mission still show zero after I traded today?
Trading missions are counted from your daily trade summary, which only exists once the trading day has closed and end-of-day reconciliation has run, roughly 5:00 PM to 6:00 PM ET. Check again after the next daily close. If it is still missing a full day later, contact support.





Why are none of my trades counting toward missions?
Check your platform first. Trading missions currently count Tradovate accounts only, and the mission card says so. Trades on Rithmic or WealthCharts do not count yet. If you are on Tradovate and a full day has passed since the close, contact support with your account details.





I have several accounts — do I earn several times?
For purchases, yes: every account you buy credits Gems, with no cap. For everything else, no. Repeating missions such as In the Game and Green Print count once for the day across all your accounts, and XP and Gems do not multiply with the number of accounts you hold.





I completed a mission but did not receive the reward
Some missions hold the reward for a manual claim. Open the Gems and level pill in the dashboard header — anything owed to you is listed there — or press Claim on the mission card itself.





Why can I not claim a reward I just earned?
If you earn a reward while end-of-day reconciliation is running, roughly 5:00 PM to 6:00 PM ET, it can take up to an hour to become claimable. Nothing is lost. Wait an hour, then check the Gems and level pill in the header again.





Why will it not let me claim a reward or open a pack?
This is almost always the Terms & Conditions prompt. You have to accept the Rewards Center terms before anything that pays out or spends will go through, and the prompt reappears whenever the terms are updated. Accept it and try again.





Why was my discount code rejected at checkout?
Check these three things, in order:



Is it already redeemed? Your Vault shows the card as Redeemed.

Are you using it on a bundle or package offer? These codes deliberately do not apply to those.

Are you signed in as the account that won it? Codes are locked to the email address that won them.



If none of those apply, contact support with the code and the product you tried to use it on.





Where do I find the code for a prize I won?
Rewards Center → Vault → click the card → Copy code. The code is used at checkout on the Tradeify site, not inside the Rewards Center.





I won a free evaluation — how do I claim it?
The same way as any other prize. Free accounts are issued as 100%-off codes: open the card in your Vault, copy the code, and apply it at checkout on the product it covers.





Do my Gems or prize codes expire?
No. Gems stay in your balance and prize codes stay in your Vault until you use them.





What happens to my Gems and prizes if I lose my accounts?
You keep them. Gems, XP, your level, and everything in your Vault are attached to your Tradeify user rather than to any trading account, so they survive an account being breached or closed.





Can I buy Gems or give Gems to another trader?
No to both. Gems are earned only, through missions, level milestones, and qualifying purchases. They cannot be bought, transferred, sold, or withdrawn.





Can I change my leaderboard name or photo?
Yes. Both are set in your Rewards Center profile, from the avatar in the top-right of the page. Setting them for the first time also completes two Getting Started missions. Names are screened automatically for inappropriate language, and you can choose not to show your name on the rankings at all.





Why are my Discord missions not counting?
Your Discord account has to be connected through the Join the Floor mission in the Rewards Center, and you need to be a member of the Tradeify Discord server. Connecting is what lets the Discord missions see your activity — being in the server without connecting is not enough. If you connect and then leave the server, your activity there can no longer be seen and those missions stop counting.





Why did my leaderboard rank drop even though I earned XP?
The board is a ranking, not a score, so other traders earning faster will pass you. Also check which view you are on: the Season board covers the current season only, while All-Time covers everything.





Getting help and reporting bugs
Two routes, depending on what is wrong:



Something in the Rewards Center looks broken — use the report-a-bug button inside the Rewards Center. During the current rollout phase, the first report from you that the team confirms as a genuine bug pays XP and Gems. Reports go straight to the product team with your context attached.

A reward, code, or mission is not behaving — contact support from your trader dashboard.



To save a round trip, include:



The mission or prize name, exactly as it appears on the card

The date and time you completed it or tried to redeem, and your timezone

The trading account and platform involved, for trading missions

The code and the product you tried to use it on, for checkout problems

A screenshot of what you are seeing



Rewards, pack prices, prize pools, and limits are set per season and change when a new season is published. The Missions and Shop tabs are always the source of truth — every card states its own reward, odds, and timing.
